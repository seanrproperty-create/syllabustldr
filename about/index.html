About

Why we built SyllabusTLDR

A tool for the first week of every semester.

Our mission

SyllabusTLDR exists to solve one very specific, very universal problem: the first week of a semester hands every student four, five, sometimes seven dense, multi-page course outlines — each formatted differently, each burying the handful of facts that actually matter (how you're graded, what's due and when, what the professor expects) inside pages of policy boilerplate. Our mission is to take that pile of PDFs and turn it into a single, pocket-sized dashboard in seconds, so students spend their first week of class planning their semester instead of decoding it.

How it actually works

When you drop a PDF or paste raw syllabus text into SyllabusTLDR, nothing leaves your device. The parsing happens entirely in your browser using client-side JavaScript — including PDF.js for text extraction — running a purpose-built pattern-matching engine that recognizes the language real syllabi are written in: grading weight tables, deadline flags like "Due," "Midterm," and "Final," and the recurring shorthand professors use for office hours, late policies, and attendance rules. The result is rendered instantly as a clean summary card: grade weights, a deadline timeline, and the key logistics, all pulled from the document you already have.

Combating academic overwhelm

Syllabus week is when time-management habits for the whole semester get set — and it's also when students are least equipped to set them, because the information they need is scattered across documents that were never designed to be compared side by side. We built SyllabusTLDR because that mismatch is fixable with better tooling, not more willpower. Give a student a clear picture of what's weighted heavily, what's coming up first, and what the non-negotiable dates are, and the rest of the semester gets easier to plan for — fewer surprise deadlines, fewer all-nighters caused by a due date that was buried on page six.

Built for speed, and for trust

There's no account to create and no document to upload to a server, because there is no server involved in the parsing at all. That's a product decision as much as a privacy one: it means the tool works the instant you land on the page, and it means the syllabus you paste in — which often includes a professor's name, contact details, and institution — never has to leave your machine to be useful to you. You can read exactly how that works in our Privacy Policy.

Who it's for

SyllabusTLDR is built for university and college students juggling multiple courses at once, but it works just as well for a single class. If you've ever opened a syllabus PDF looking for one number — "how much is the final worth?" — and had to scroll past three pages to find it, this tool is for you.

What's next

We're actively expanding SyllabusTLDR's language support so students studying in English-medium programs around the world can use the interface in their own language, and we're building out a library of guides on the blog covering syllabus literacy, deadline planning, and semester-long study strategy. Have a feature request or found a syllabus format that trips the parser up? Get in touch — we read every message.
